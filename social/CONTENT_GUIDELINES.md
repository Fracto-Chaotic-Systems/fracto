# Content guidelines

## Public promise

Fracto explores the hidden dynamics inside the Mandelbrot Set, where orbitals
become patterns, colors, frequencies, and unexpected structure.

The account should make a reader curious before asking them to understand the
mathematics. Images, motion, sound, and metaphor are entry points; the research
is the substance behind them.

## Established voice

- Write in the first person when describing the investigation: “I noticed…”,
  “I wondered…”, and “I’m testing…”.
- Prefer plain language and concrete images to unexplained technical terms.
- Use poetic comparisons—music, waves, fruit, candy, geysers, landscapes—but
  explain what observation inspired the comparison.
- Treat the Mandelbrot Set with wonder without presenting metaphor as proof.
- Ask genuine questions and invite the audience to look closely.
- Keep the tone enthusiastic, patient, and exploratory rather than promotional.

## Core themes

1. **Inside the Set** — the interior is not empty or merely a region to exclude;
   it contains dynamics worth studying.
2. **Orbitals** — repeating paths, cardinality, and the relationship between a
   point and its cycle.
3. **Mathematics translated into experience** — orbital cardinality as color,
   rhythm, frequency, motion, or sound.
4. **Visual phenomena** — minibrots, nodes, geysers, intersections, and other
   recurring artifacts.
5. **Research in progress** — discoveries, uncertainty, performance constraints,
   failed approaches, and the next experiment.

## Editorial guardrails

Use “shows,” “suggests,” or “we are investigating” for observations that have
not been formally established. Reserve “proves” and “discovered” for results
that have been checked and documented independently.

When a result is provisional, say what would change the conclusion. A useful
pattern is:

> The rendering suggests **X**. We are testing whether **Y** explains it.

Do not imply that color is an intrinsic property of the mathematical set. Explain
that Fracto assigns color according to a chosen dynamic or visualization rule.

## Format guidance

- Short posts should make one point, use one image or one clear question, and
  end with an invitation when appropriate.
- Longer explanations should be a numbered thread: hook, visual evidence,
  explanation, interpretation, caveat, and question.
- Use two to four relevant hashtags, usually selected from `#Mandelbrot`,
  `#Fractals`, `#MathArt`, `#SciArt`, `#MathIsBeautiful`, and `#ChaosTheory`.
- Write specific alt text describing both the visual subject and the scientific
  idea represented.
- Keep recurring series names stable so readers can recognize them.

## Current campaign objective

Build a curious, technically credible audience that follows the investigation
over time. The immediate goal is not maximum posting volume; it is learning which
visuals, metaphors, and explanations cause people to ask meaningful questions,
visit the profile, and return for the next result.
